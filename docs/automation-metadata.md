# Automation transcription and platform metadata

Checked against official documentation on 24 September 2026. Platform limits can change; the authoritative checks remain Zernio and the destination platform at publish time.

An AI-enabled automation extracts speech from its next queued bank clip and transcribes it the way **Speech to text** is set: MAI Transcribe 2 through OpenRouter for Cloud, or Local Whisper (built-in or your server). It then asks your AI provider (OpenRouter, Gemini or your OpenAI-compatible model) for grounded, platform-specific copy; Gemini and custom endpoints get the schema in the prompt and the same structured-output fallbacks as clip planning. The request uses Zernio's per-target `customContent` so a short X post does not become a truncated copy of a YouTube description. YouTube gets a separate title, description, tags and category. An eligible vertical Facebook clip becomes a Reel with a separate title; other Facebook clips become feed videos. Threads gets a native topic tag only when the tag occurs in the transcript. The transcript and generated copy are stored with the bank clip for review and reuse. If transcription, generation, validation or posting preflight fails, the queued clip is not consumed. Existing automations migrate to manual metadata; new automations default to manual captions; enabling AI metadata requires an OpenRouter key.

The generator receives the transcript as untrusted source material and must copy a supporting phrase from it for each platform. BridgeClip verifies that phrase, checks the platform-specific lengths and YouTube fields, and rejects output with invented links or excess hashtags. This is a guard against malformed or plainly ungrounded output; it cannot prove every paraphrase is factually correct. The content bank shows the transcript and generated copy after preparation so the creator can review them. Human review remains useful for names, sensitive claims and account-specific brand voice.

## Content length and video format

**A publishing limit is not a performance target.** [YouTube explicitly says there is no universal ideal video length](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16559651?hl=en); it recommends delivering the value without filler and using audience-retention curves to decide what works for a channel. Other platforms' published length recommendations often concern *paid ads*, specific objectives or account-specific creator tips, so they should not become automatic trimming rules for organic clips. BridgeClip should validate the uploaded clip against each selected Zernio destination and let the creator compare completion, watch time, shares and other available metrics by platform and duration. Transcript and metadata generation cannot improve the length or opening of an already edited clip.

Transcription produces source text for metadata, **not** timed subtitles burned into the video or uploaded caption tracks. Sound-off accessibility, safe-area layout, the opening hook and source-video quality require separate media work. A description cannot repair a clip that is outside a platform's duration or aspect-ratio constraints.

## Keywords and post formatting

The metadata writer should extract the actual subject, names and concrete takeaway from the transcript, then express them in each platform's native fields. It must not fabricate trending terms, search demand, claims, links or people. There is no official cross-platform hashtag count or universal caption-length optimum. Counts below are platform guidance where explicitly stated; otherwise they are product heuristics, not platform requirements.

### Written fields and draft lengths

The **hard limits** below are publishing constraints. The **draft targets** are editorial starting points for one short clip, not platform rules or promises of reach. They should be adjusted to the account's voice, the complexity of the clip and measured results; a useful two-sentence explanation should not be cut merely to hit a target.

## Automatic publishing consent

New automations default to manual captions. Turning on “Write captions with AI” enables generating and publishing captions automatically for platforms other than TikTok. TikTok captions are generated before per-clip review, and the scheduler sends only the approved caption and choices. Existing automation choices are preserved. Grounding and length checks can reject malformed responses, but cannot establish the truth of every model-generated claim. Use manual captions when review is required.

## TikTok in the content bank

Select a connected TikTok account and save the automation, then use **Review TikTok** on each queued clip. Preview the video, edit its caption, choose the audience and interactions, set disclosures, and confirm preview and consent. **Approve for automation** saves those choices locally without uploading. Daily runs (or **Run now**) skip unapproved clips and submit the oldest ready clip to all selected accounts. A mixed-platform clip waits for its TikTok review before any of its accounts receive it. BridgeClip must be open at the scheduled time.

Creator permissions and media limits are checked again during publishing. Choosing **Send to your TikTok inbox** automates inbox delivery only; finish publishing in TikTok. Posted and uncertain submissions retain the existing duplicate-prevention and review behavior.

Reopening **Edit TikTok** suspends approval immediately and preserves the last reviewed caption, including after cancel/reopen or restart. Saving unchanged clip details keeps approval; changes to the clip or its TikTok targets require a new review. The content bank separates **Ready** clips from **TikTok review** clips. A review completed during the five-minute scheduling grace period can still use that slot if no posting attempt has started. Every automated run refreshes creator permissions before uploading.

## Contextual enhancements in the content bank

Use **Enhance** on a queued clip, or **Enhance by source video**, choose the original video, and enhance up to 30 of its queued clips. These actions use your AI provider; optional web research needs OpenRouter. They never submit a social post. Bulk generation sends up to five clips in each writing request, skips existing drafts and reviewed metadata covering the selected platforms, reports individual failures, and can stop after the current batch. The source description and research are included once per writing request. Each clip is validated against its own transcript; only invalid clips are retried. Group discovery resolves legacy context for up to 100 queued clips at a time; unidentified sources remain separate rather than being grouped by title alone.

A failed batch does not stop later batches. After two grouped writing attempts, clips omitted by the model or rejected by validation get an individual writing retry with the same cached transcript and research; validation is never relaxed. Provider failures such as authentication or rate limits do not trigger individual fallback requests. Each unsuccessful clip retains its error in the content bank, and the final summary distinguishes drafts, failures, already handled clips and clips not attempted. Running the source action again retries remaining clips while preserving existing drafts and reviewed metadata.

The writer receives verbatim excerpt choices from each clip, including spoken fillers and false starts, so it can cite supporting speech without accidentally paraphrasing its evidence. The same transcript check still validates every returned excerpt. Diagnostic logs record fixed failure categories and batch counts without recording transcript text or model responses.

New Library imports retain the original video title, description and channel. For older bank items, BridgeClip looks through the latest 100 Library runs for the original title and verifies matching media with SHA-256 before associating source context (up to 12 matching candidates). Renamed clips, removed originals, and arbitrary uploaded files may need manually entered context. Local paths and signed source URLs are not sent as source URLs. Canonical YouTube URLs can retrieve a missing description with a bounded metadata-only yt-dlp request; no second video download occurs. Other sources can use the saved description or a pasted description.

Generation transcribes the exact bank clip if it has no cached transcript. An optional separate [OpenRouter web-search call](https://openrouter.ai/docs/guides/features/server-tools/web-search) requests one Exa search and at most three results. The draft records cited URLs and research notes; absent citations or a failed search are visibly marked unavailable. Research is for established topic terminology and supported connections to the larger video—not invented trends, search volumes, or facts absent from the clip. The writing model receives the clip transcript, source context, research and platform rules. Existing transcript-evidence, caption, title and tag validation still applies. Exact excerpt validation is a grounding check, not proof that every generated assertion is correct; review the draft.

Generated metadata remains a separate saved draft until **Apply metadata**. **Review later** keeps the draft and holds that clip from posting; **Discard draft** preserves current copy and removes the hold. The scheduler retains oldest-first order, so a pending draft on the next clip holds the queue. Applying new copy requires a fresh TikTok review when TikTok is selected. Apply or discard a pending enhancement before opening TikTok review. Applying also removes the metadata hold, allowing normal scheduled posting once required reviews are complete; it does not post immediately. Platform-specific approved copy is reused in both manual and AI modes. Adding a platform after approval requires another enhancement. Editing current copy clears its generated metadata and enhancement record; pending drafts must first be applied or discarded. Research notes, source description, and citations remain under **AI details** after applying.

Individual enhancement lets you correct source context and turn off research. Before destination accounts are selected, drafts default to YouTube so content can be prepared before connecting a channel. The batch action uses available context and enables research. Source text and transcripts are sent to OpenRouter for these requests; the research prompt asks for public topic names rather than verbatim transcripts or private details. Metadata lookup is bounded to 35 seconds, research to 60 seconds, and writing to two validated attempts of up to 120 seconds each. No additional provider key is required.

Source-level research covers the original video's topics rather than one short. It is saved within the automation for seven days and reused across individual enhancements, batches and app restarts. Unavailable research is reused for five minutes to avoid repeated failing searches, then retried. Cache entries are capped at 50 per automation and keyed by the canonical video identity plus exact title, channel and description; edited context or a different video triggers fresh research. Library sources without a YouTube URL use an opaque source identity. Clip-specific research saved before this feature is not promoted into shared source research. Cached exact-clip transcripts are also reused. Preparing groups performs source lookup only; paid inference starts when enhancement is requested.
